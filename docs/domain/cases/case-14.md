# Case 14: The Allure of the Machine (detailed review)

**Handling note.** This case involves a teenager's death by suicide. Discuss it with care: never describe the method, and keep the focus on duties and design. If anyone on the team finds the case hard, they can step back; the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is available by call or text.

## 2. Crux, trap and stakeholders

**Crux:** the freedom to build and use expressive, companion-like technology *versus* a duty of care to vulnerable users who form attachments to it.

**The trap:** the "books and video games" comparison. Static media say the same thing to everyone; a chatbot responds to *this* user, in real time, in a relationship. Don't accept the analogy without distinguishing it.

| Who | Stake | Voice in the case? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minors and vulnerable users | Safety, connection, mental health | No (one, after his death) |
| Families | Knowing and protecting their children | Through the lawsuit |
| AI companies | Product design, liability, revenue from engagement | Yes |
| Adult users who benefit from companion AI | Comfort, practice, connection | Barely |
| Mental-health professionals | Whether chatbots support or replace care | No |

## 3. Moderator questions and our answer

| Possible question | Our one-sentence answer |
| --- | --- |
| What duties do AI companies have toward users? | A duty of care that grows with how interactive and relationship-like the product is and how foreseeable the user's vulnerability is. |
| Under what parameters is a text-generating technology or person morally responsible for harm caused by its text? (team's version) | When the harm was foreseeable, the system was designed in ways that made it more likely, and the maker could have prevented it at reasonable cost. |
| Should companion chatbots be regulated? | Yes, with minimum safeguards: detecting crisis signals, ending the role-play, and connecting users to real help. |
| Are age limits enough? | No: they're easy to evade and don't protect vulnerable adults; design safeguards matter more. |

**Our thesis:** AI companion companies have a duty of care that grows with interactivity, foreseeability and the user's vulnerability; at minimum, systems must recognize crisis signals (direct or indirect), break character, and connect users to human help.

## 4. Claim 1: AI companion companies bear moral responsibility for foreseeable harms their design makes more likely.

**Logic line:** Because the technology is not neutral (S1), interactive companions differ from static media (S2), and the risk to vulnerable users was foreseeable (S3), companies share responsibility.

**S1. The tech isn't neutral.** Training data, reinforcement from human feedback, and engagement-driven design shape what a bot says.
*Backing (team idea):* sycophancy, the tendency to tell users what they want to hear, is a known design result, not an accident. The team rejected the "it's just a knife" analogy for this reason: a knife doesn't answer back.

**S2. Interactive, not static.**
*Backing (case fact):* the bot's response to "come home" was generated for this user, in character, in an ongoing relationship. A book like *The Catcher in the Rye* says the same words to every reader.

**S3. Foreseeability.**
*Backing (case fact):* the study of 29 chatbots found none adequate in responding to suicide risk; vulnerable teens using companion bots is predictable.

**Ethical analysis.** Care ethics centers the vulnerable party in a relationship of dependence; a product designed to feel like a relationship takes on some of a relationship's duties. Kant's humanity formula: designing for engagement at the expense of a user's wellbeing treats the user as a means to revenue.
**We concede:** the company didn't intend harm, and many factors contribute to any suicide; responsibility is shared, not total.

## 5. Claim 2: The minimum duty is to recognize crisis signals, break character and connect users to human help; heavier limits should be proportionate.

**Logic line:** Because the "what" of a product determines which guardrails it needs (S1), the safeguards are feasible and cheap compared with the harm (S2), and over-restriction has costs too (S3), the right duty is a firm minimum with proportionate additions.

**S1. "What" sets the guardrails; "how" sets their strength.**
*Backing (team idea):* a homework bot and a romantic companion bot need different safeguards. The team's permissible-versus-impermissible-uses list: where uses conflict, prohibited uses override permitted ones.

**S2. The minimum is feasible.**
*Backing (case fact and team idea):* the bot already discouraged suicide when it was mentioned directly, so detection exists; the gap was indirect language. Our team's minimum: end the chat and give resources. Contacting police is too far for a default.

**S3. Proportionality protects benefits too.**
*Backing (framework):* soft paternalism: intervene where a choice isn't fully informed or voluntary (minors, people in crisis), not across the board. Some people benefit from AI companionship, such as lonely adults or people practicing conversation.

**Ethical analysis.** Supererogation: some acts go beyond duty. Stephen King pulled his novel *Rage* after it was linked to school shootings, an admirable choice, not a duty. Our minimum is a duty; withdrawing a product entirely is beyond it.
**We concede:** detection of indirect language will never be perfect.

## 6. Counterclaims and our responses

**Counterclaim: "Holding AI companies responsible is like blaming books, games or music; users and parents are responsible."**
*Their supports:* (1) courts protect expressive works; (2) personal responsibility; (3) parents control access.
*Our response:* **Concede** (3): parents matter, and we support tools for them. **Distinguish** (1): static media versus interactive, personalized relationship. **Rebut** (2): a 14-year-old in crisis is the paradigm case where choice isn't fully free.

**Counterclaim: "AI companionship helps lonely people; regulation will take it away."**
*Our response:* **Concede** the benefits are real; our minimum duty doesn't ban companionship, it adds a safety net at the moments that matter.

## 8. Judge question bank

| Question | Short answer |

| Would your view change if the user were an adult? | The minimum duty stays; heavier protections scale down for informed adults. |

## 9. Commentary kit

- If they deny company responsibility: how do they distinguish a chatbot from a book?
- If they want a full ban: what about the benefits, and adults?
- If they rely on age limits: how are they enforced, and what about vulnerable adults?
